A question we

How can we use the resources we have to help others as much as possible?

What is effective altruism?

Effective altruism combines a concern for others with a willingness to compare different ways of helping. It asks where our time, money, and skills can make the biggest difference.

That means looking at evidence, being clear about uncertainty, and reconsidering our views when we learn something new. It also means taking people and animals seriously even when their lives are very different from ours.

Our group gives students a place to explore these ideas. You don’t need to agree with every argument, or have a career plan, to take part.

Some of the questions we discuss

What deserves more attention?

Global health & development

How can we most effectively reduce preventable illness and poverty? We discuss approaches such as malaria prevention and direct cash transfers.

Animal welfare

How should we account for animal suffering, and what can changes in farming, policy, and our own choices achieve?

AI safety & governance

How can technical research and public policy help ensure increasingly capable AI systems are safe?

Biosecurity

What would make the world better prepared to prevent and respond to severe pandemics?

The intro fellowship

Eight weeks.
A small group.
Big questions.

A facilitated reading and discussion group on effective altruism: cause prioritization, global health, animal welfare, and risks to the long-term future.

Plan for around two hours a week, including reading and discussion. No prior knowledge is needed.
